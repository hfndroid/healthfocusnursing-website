The three plan types

Every NDIS plan is managed in one of three ways, and it determines which providers you can use. Self-managed means funding comes to you and you pay providers directly, which gives the most flexibility. Plan-managed means a plan manager handles invoices on your behalf. NDIA-managed means the NDIA pays providers directly, and importantly, only NDIS registered providers can be used.

Health Focus Nursing is a registered provider, Organisation ID 4050159566, so all three plan types can use us. If you are NDIA-managed, registration is not a detail, it is the thing that determines whether a provider is available to you at all.

Which budget pays for what

Community nursing, including wound care, catheter management, medication administration and bowel care, comes from Core Supports at NDIS item 0114. Personal care sits at 0107, domestic assistance at 0120, and social and community participation at 0125.

A continence assessment is different. It is funded from Capacity Building, specifically Improved Daily Living, because it builds the evidence base for supports rather than delivering daily care. This catches people out regularly, and it is worth checking that this category has funding in it before an assessment is booked.

High-intensity daily personal activities, covering tracheostomy management, ventilator support, enteral feeding, complex bowel care and severe dysphagia management, require both funding and a provider registered to deliver them. We hold that registration.

What it costs

All services are billed at current NDIS Price Guide rates. For eligible participants with the relevant funding available, there is no out-of-pocket cost. We do not charge establishment fees or exit fees.

If the funding is not in your plan, we tell you before starting. Beginning a service that cannot be billed helps nobody, and it leaves participants with an invoice they did not expect.
