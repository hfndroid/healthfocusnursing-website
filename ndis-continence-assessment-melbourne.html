What is an NDIS continence assessment?

An NDIS continence assessment is a structured clinical review that looks at how a bladder or bowel condition affects someone's daily life, and what support or equipment might help. The assessment is conducted by a registered nurse who talks through medical history, current continence management and the practical impact on everyday activities like sleeping, going out and personal care.

At Health Focus Nursing, the assessment is always led by an AHPRA-registered nurse rather than a general support worker, which means the clinical detail in the report holds up when reviewed by NDIS planners. The formal written report that follows covers the participant's continence history, current management strategies, functional impact and clear recommendations, often including product or equipment suggestions.

The finished report links directly back to NDIS funding categories, giving planners the evidence they need to approve continence-related supports. It is suited to participants of any age who are experiencing bladder or bowel challenges connected to their disability, and it is equally useful whether you are applying for funding for the first time or renewing an existing plan.

Why a registered nurse-led assessment matters

Continence needs are a clinical matter, not just a comfort issue, and the person assessing them needs the training to match. A registered nurse understands the physiology behind bladder and bowel dysfunction, can identify when a presentation needs further medical review, and knows how to document findings in language that NDIS planners recognise and trust.

This clinical grounding is a big part of why Health Focus Nursing reports are accepted the first time they are submitted. Our AHPRA registration means every assessment is backed by verifiable professional standards, ongoing clinical education and accountability to a national regulator. For participants, that translates into a report that is thorough, accurate and written specifically to satisfy the requirements of an NDIS funding decision, rather than a generic template filled in by someone without a clinical background.

How is an NDIS continence assessment funded?

A continence assessment is generally funded under the Capacity Building support category, specifically Improved Daily Living. This category is designed to help participants build the skills and access the supports they need to manage daily tasks, and continence assessments fall squarely within that scope.

The assessment can be accessed by participants regardless of how their plan is managed, whether self managed, plan managed or NDIA managed. All services are billed at current NDIS Price Guide rates, and for eligible participants with available funding in the right category, there is no out-of-pocket cost.
