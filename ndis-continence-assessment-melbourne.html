What is an NDIS continence assessment?

If a bladder or bowel condition is affecting daily life, the NDIS will usually want a formal assessment before funding continence supports. That assessment is a structured clinical review of what is happening, how much it affects everyday activities, and what products, equipment or strategies would help.

At Health Focus Nursing the assessment is always led by an AHPRA-registered nurse rather than a support worker. That matters because the report has to hold up when a planner reads it. The nurse reviews medical and continence history, carries out a physical assessment, looks at the practical impact on sleeping, going out and personal care, and then writes it up in language an NDIS delegate recognises.

The finished report links findings directly to NDIS funding categories. It suits participants of any age whose bladder or bowel difficulties relate to their disability, whether you are seeking funding for the first time or preparing for a plan review.

Why a nurse-led assessment gets accepted

Continence is a clinical matter, not a comfort issue. A registered nurse understands the physiology behind bladder and bowel dysfunction, recognises when something needs further medical review, and knows how to document findings so a planner can act on them.

That is the practical difference. AHPRA registration means every assessment sits behind verifiable professional standards, ongoing clinical education and accountability to a national regulator. For participants it means a report written specifically to satisfy an NDIS funding decision, rather than a template filled in by someone without clinical training.

How it is funded

Continence assessments are funded under Capacity Building, Improved Daily Living. That category exists to help participants build skills and access supports for daily tasks, and continence sits squarely within it.

You can access an assessment whether your plan is self-managed, plan-managed or NDIA-managed. We bill at current NDIS Price Guide rates, so for eligible participants with funding available there is no out-of-pocket cost.
